Three Tasks, Three Groups

Each stage of the curriculum has a symmetry group, and each group leaves a fingerprint that can be measured: in the flow field, in the number and placement of its fixed points, and in the population structure of the low-rank vectors. This note takes the stages in order. For each one: the group, what it forces, and what a network trained under that constraint actually looks like. Every number here is measured on the networks named in the captions. The full derivations, with the group-theory definitions and theorems recalled, are in the companion Klein Group Derivations.

§ 1

Setup: what a symmetry acts on

The network has N units, a rank-2 recurrent matrix Wrec = m nT/N, input weights Win with a per-unit bias b, a gain g, and a transfer function φ applied unit by unit. Its state collapses onto two overlaps, κj = njTr/N: κ₀ is the memory, κ₁ the decision. A fixed point of the network under a constant input x is a fixed point of the planar map

Ψ(κ; x) = (1/N) nT φ(g(mκ + Winx + b)),     F = Ψ − κ F is the field drawn in every flow figure. Its stable zeros are the wells; the line κ₁ = 0 is the lick line. The goal is a pair of memory wells below it. Input noise is isotropic across channels, so averaging F over it changes nothing below.

A task symmetry is a relabeling of the stimuli, with the response relabeled to match, that leaves the task unchanged. In the network it has to act in three places at once: a permutation P of the units, a permutation S of the input channels, and a 2×2 sign matrix D on the κ-plane. The network is equivariant under the symmetry when

P m = m D     P n = n D     P Win = Win S     P b = b four conditions: the write vectors, the read vectors, the input columns, and the bias. All the permutations below are involutions, so P−1 = PT = P and D−1 = D.

Three lines then give the whole engine. Substitute Dκ into the drive: m Dκ + Winx + b = P(mκ + WinS−1x + b). φ acts unit by unit, so it commutes with P. Read out with n: since nTP = (P n)T = D nT,

F(Dκ; x) = D F(κ; S−1x) the field under a stimulus, reflected, equals the field under the relabeled stimulus. With no input the fixed-point set is mapped onto itself by D: wells come in orbits.

Orbits have three possible sizes, and this is the counting rule used throughout. A point with both coordinates nonzero has as many images as the group has elements. A point on an axis is fixed by the reflection in that axis and has half as many. The origin is fixed by everything.

§ 2 · DPA

The memory task and the Klein four-group

Sample s ∈ {A, B}, a delay, test t ∈ {C, D}; lick if the pair matches (A with C, B with D). As a truth function, with A and C coded 0,

r = ¬(s ⊕ t) a parity, not a property of either odor alone. This one line fixes the group.

Flip s, flip t, or flip both. Those three relabelings, with the identity, close into the Klein four-group V = {1, σ1, σ2, σ3}: every element is its own inverse and the product of any two is the third. Flipping both preserves the parity, so the response is unchanged. Flipping one inverts it, and an inverted response is a minus sign on κ₁. The memory coordinate carries the sample, so flipping s is a minus sign on κ₀.

ElementRelabelingResponseAction on κForces on the memory wells
σ1A↔B and C↔Dpreserved D1 = diag(−1, +1)mirror images in κ₀, at one common height
σ2 = σ1σ3A↔B alonematch ↔ nonmatch D2 = −Ipartners through the origin: opposite heights
σ3C↔D alonematch ↔ nonmatch D3 = diag(+1, −1)closed under κ₁ ↦ −κ₁: quadruples, or pinned on the line

The DPA objective is invariant under all three. Its terms are the sample hold on κ₀ (+1 after A, −1 after B), a two-sided pairing target on κ₁ (+1 on match, −1 on nonmatch), and a two-sided zero pin before the sample. There is no one-sided term and nothing that distinguishes licking from not licking beyond the sign. So gradient descent on this objective has no reason of its own to prefer one element of V over another.

The scheme

Put the A-odor well at (+a, w). Each element demands a partner, and the demand is the story.

σ2 — inversion

one down forces one up

The partner of (a, w) is (−a, −w). Two wells both below the line is not difficult under σ2; it is impossible.

σ1 — pair exchange

one height for both

The partner is (−a, w). The two wells share a single w, whatever it is: they sink together or not at all.

the whole group V

quadruples, or two on the line

Closed under both reflections. A well off both axes brings three images (filled). A memory made of exactly two wells must therefore sit on the line (open).

The counting form of the same statement is the one that can be checked on a flow field. Under σ1 alone an attractor on the κ₁ axis is its own image and may stand alone, so the number of attractors can be odd. Under the whole group every attractor off the origin has an even orbit, so the number is even and the set is symmetric about both axes.

What the initialization provides — exactly, and why

The initialization does not impose a symmetry on the units, yet the inversion holds at initialization exactly. The reason is specific to our transfer function and worth stating precisely. φ is the Gaussian cumulative, φ(u) = ½ + ½ erf(u/√2): a constant plus an odd function. Write ⟨n⟩ for the mean of n over units. Then, with no input,

Ψ(κ) + Ψ(−κ) = ⟨n⟩ + (1/N) Σi ni [Φ(g(mi·κ + bi)) − Φ(g(mi·κ − bi))] Φ the Gaussian cumulative. The even part of the field is a constant, ⟨n⟩, plus a term that is odd in the bias. At initialization n is built with zero mean and b = 0, so the right-hand side vanishes identically: F(−κ) = −F(κ) for any draw of m and n, at any N.

Two remarks. First, this is not the general mechanism. For a transfer function with no such structure, such as a rectifier, the field is odd only if the unit population is itself symmetric under (m, n) ↦ (−m, −n), which a finite Gaussian sample is not. Second, for an odd φ such as tanh the constant is zero, so the readout mean cannot break the inversion at all and only the bias can. The tanh sweeps that preceded this transfer function kept their wells symmetric to the extent that the bias term stayed small, which was checked at the time (Ψ(0) ≈ 0); with a zero bias a tanh memory can only be a quadruple, never two wells below the line.

Training breaks the inversion through exactly the two parameters the identity names: the mean of the readout, and the bias. Measured as ‖F(Dκ) − D F(κ)‖ ⁄ ‖F‖ over the disk |κ| ≤ 1.5, on the eight seeds of the final recipe:

σ2 at initialization, 8 seeds0.000 in every seed σ1, σ3 at initialization0.03 – 0.12 σ2 after DPA0.11 – 0.95 σ1 after DPA0.09 – 0.70

The other two elements hold at initialization only to the extent that a finite Gaussian sample is symmetric under flipping one mode, a few percent at N = 1024. So the initial network is equivariant under the whole group: exactly for the inversion, approximately for the rest.

An accidental extra: the continuous symmetry

The task asks only for the discrete group, but the initialization gives more. Both modes are built with the same spread, the same m–n correlation and no cross-mode correlation, so the empirical covariance of the four numbers per unit is isotropic between modes. Then the field commutes with every rotation of the plane, O(2), and above the critical overlap the memory states form a ring with a tangential eigenvalue of exactly zero. Whatever anisotropy the finite sample carries breaks O(2) to the discrete group, and because the field is odd only even harmonics appear: the leading one is cos 2θ, whose four critical points on the ring are two attractors and two saddles at right angles.

Training under the constraint: σ1 alone, or the whole group

A network can be held on the equivariant subspace during training by projecting m, n and Win back onto it after every optimizer step. The two natural choices differ in exactly one way, and that way is about the lick line. Tying σ1 forces the two memory wells to share a height and says nothing about what the height is. Tying the whole group adds σ3, and with it closure under reflection through the line: a memory of exactly two wells is pinned at κ₁ = 0, and any extra attractor must arrive with its reflection.

… and at the level of m and n

A network that realizes V has its units in four blocks indexed by two bits (a, b), the memory mode carrying one parity and the decision mode the other:

m₀ = (−1)aμ₀,   n₀ = (−1)aν₀   |   m₁ = (−1)bμ₁,   n₁ = (−1)bν₁ within a block m₀ and n₀ share a sign and so do m₁ and n₁; the two parities are independent. The cross-overlap n₀Tm₁ sums (−1)a+b over the four blocks and vanishes identically.

The constraint is a statement about individual units, so it can also be checked one unit at a time: take a unit in the first block, apply the group element, and compare the value the constraint predicts for the image unit with the value the network has there.

The DPA result

The memory task is invariant under the Klein four-group, and a network trained on it starts out equivariant under it. Held to the group, the network keeps its two memory wells on the lick line and its four unit populations in an exact sign lattice, with the cross-overlap between memory and decision identically zero. Left free, it keeps the lattice as a trend, its wells stay near the line, and it breaks the group in a seed-dependent direction: some seeds give up the inversion, some the pair exchange. Which one they give up is what the next two stages inherit.

A control: tying against the same initialization untied

An earlier experiment on a different base (λ = 14, ρ = 0.8, and a deliberate downward bias ⟨n₁⟩ = −0.1 in the initialization) compared σ1 tied through DPA against the identical initialization with the tie removed. The bias is what places the wells below the line here, so this is not evidence about depth. It is evidence about the tie.

§ 3 · GNG

The rule task adds one symmetry and breaks two

The rule stage introduces stimuli the memory group never saw, go and nogo, and with them a new candidate symmetry τ: exchange go with nogo and invert the response. On the κ-plane τ acts as (+κ₀, −κ₁), the same action as σ3.

But τ is a symmetry of the abstract rule, not of the objective we train. Go must drive κ₁ above a positive threshold; nogo only has to keep it at or below zero, a one-sided cost with no counterpart above. The asymmetry is deliberate, it reflects the asymmetry of the animal's errors, and in the language of physics it is an explicit symmetry-breaking field with a definite sign.

What breaks, and why

Every element whose action carries a minus sign on κ₁, which is σ2, σ3 and τ, is incompatible with the rule stage. σ1, which leaves κ₁ alone and does not touch the go, nogo and cue channels, is untouched. The reason is sharper than the one-sided cost: a network equivariant under any of those three cannot hear the rule at all.

Take σ3. It does not exchange go with anything, so the third equivariance condition says P wgo = wgo: the go column takes the same value on a unit and on its image. The decision read vector changes sign between them. Summing over the pairs,

n₁Twgo = Σpairs (ν₁ − ν₁) wgo = 0,   and likewise for nogo and the cue the same holds under σ2. Under σ1 it is n₀ that flips, so the rule stimuli have zero overlap with the memory readout instead, which is what one wants.

So the go and nogo inputs have no first-order projection onto the decision axis while σ3 holds. To learn the rule the network must break it, and the measurement below shows it doing so, from an exact zero.

Which parameters carry the break

The rule stage freezes the memory mode and the memory input columns. What is free is the decision mode, the bias, and the go, nogo and cue columns, and the table above shows where the rule goes: into the input columns, which the autonomous field never sees. This is why the field itself barely changes during the rule stage. Its inversion residual in the free networks moves from 0.11–0.95 after DPA to 0.14–0.90 after GNG, and in the released networks from 0.02–0.05 to 0.07–0.13. The memory wells stay where they were, on the line. Nothing in a go or nogo trial visits a memory well, so nothing in the rule objective prices the height of one. That has to wait for a trial in which the memory and the lick cost meet, which is the Dual stage.

Two of the four released networks show something the table also records: a leak of the nogo input onto the memory readout, +0.9 and +1.1, which is forbidden under σ1; in one of them (seed 2) it shows as a jump of the pair-exchange residual to 0.39 already after GNG. The free curriculum shows the same leak in several seeds.

What the rule stage does to the inputs

The third equivariance condition is a statement about which input may correlate with which readout, and both sides are vectors that can be read off the trained network directly.

Two further observations from the rule stage, on the λ scan rather than the recipe. If the rule is stored as a κ₁ memory, with a hold during the delay, the field grows a second attractor pair on the decision axis, at (0, ±1), alongside the memory pair at (±1, 0): four wells on two axes, for two different reasons. If the rule is carried as a transient, with no hold, the memory pair survives untouched and the rule never becomes an attractor. The final recipe uses the second.

§ 4 · Dual

What the solution of the dual task is

The dual task interleaves both, with a response cue. In group terms the solution is short to state: keep the pair exchange, lose the rest. … and at the level of m and n

The population structure survives the whole curriculum: the same four clusters, the same sign lattice, at all three checkpoints. What changes is the scale of the decision loading and the small off-diagonal coupling. The group fixes the form of the solution; training sets its magnitudes.

§ 5

Worked example: the whole group, in four blocks

σ1 alone needs two blocks of units. The full group needs four, because V acts freely on generic units: each prototype unit generates an orbit of four. Label units by a prototype index j and two bits (a, b), with P1 flipping a and P3 flipping b; their product flips both, which is σ2.

Apply P1m = m·diag(−1, +1) component-wise: the first column must change sign when a flips, the second must not. Then P3m = m·diag(+1, −1), which flips b. Together they leave one free vector per mode per prototype, the parities of §2. For the inputs, σ3 leaves A and B alone so wA cannot depend on b, while σ1 exchanges A with B across a: two free vectors α, β. Both generators swap C with D, so wC depends only on whether the bits agree: two more, γ and δ. Go, nogo, the cue and the bias are touched by nothing, so they are shared by all four blocks.

Block (a, b)m₀, n₀m₁, n₁ wAwBwCwDgo / nogo / cue / b
(0, 0)+μ₀, +ν₀+μ₁, +ν₁αβγδshared
(0, 1)+μ₀, +ν₀−μ₁, −ν₁αβδγshared
(1, 0)−μ₀, −ν₀+μ₁, +ν₁βαδγshared
(1, 1)−μ₀, −ν₀−μ₁, −ν₁βαγδshared

Check one entry against the conditions. Under σ3, block (a, b) maps to (a, ¬b) and C to D, so wC on the image must equal wD on the original: wC(a, ¬b) is γ when a = ¬b, that is when a ≠ b, and wD(a, b) is γ exactly then. The whole network is ten vectors of length N/4 plus the shared columns. Two identities are worth doing by hand:

n₀Tm₁ ⁄ N = ⟨ν₀μ₁⟩ · ¼ Σa,b (−1)a+b = 0 n₀Tm₀ ⁄ N = ⟨ν₀μ₀⟩ · ¼ Σa,b (−1)2a = ⟨ν₀μ₀⟩ the cross-overlap vanishes because the two parities ride on different bits; the diagonal overlap survives because a sign times itself is one. Likewise n₁Twgo = 0, the deafness of §3.

That cross-overlap is the coupling between memory and decision, the quantity that has tracked memory retention across every manipulation in the project. In this parametrization it is zero not because training drove it there, but because there is no way to write it down.

Two practical notes. At initialization the orbit is copied from the first block, which keeps each unit's magnitude; the overlaps then become those of the prototype block rather than of the whole sample (5.8 and 7.3 in one seed built for λ = 7). During training the orbit is projected, a signed average over it, so all four blocks feed the gradient. And N must be a multiple of four, with an effective population of N/4, worth remembering when comparing against a free network whose covariance is estimated from four times as many independent draws.

§ 6

What the group does and does not explain

It explains the form of everything measured here. Why wells come in pairs and never singly; why a corrugated ring has two attractors and two saddles; why the cross-overlap between memory and decision is the quantity that tracks retention; why the trained population splits into four clusters in a sign lattice; why a network that respects the memory group cannot hear the rule; why the memory wells could not both descend until a trial priced the lick at the well itself; and why, once they descend, they descend together.

It does not fix magnitudes. How deep the wells go, how large the overlaps grow, how fast the slow direction becomes, which way a free seed breaks the group: those are set by the task's amplitude targets, by the strength of the breaking field and by the initial draw, and they have to be measured.

The practical consequence is a single design rule. Give the network the symmetry that relates the two memory states, and withhold the one that relates lick to no-lick. The geometry that was a lucky outcome in most seeds becomes the only solution available.

What changed in this revision

  • The inversion at initialization is exact because φ is a constant plus an odd function and both ⟨n⟩ and the bias start at zero, not because of an ensemble symmetry that a finite sample does not have. Measured 0.000 in all eight seeds; the ensemble argument is kept as the general case.
  • The bias is a fourth equivariance condition. The projection used for the published runs did not tie it; the low-rank structure is exact and the field carries a 1–5% residual. The projection now ties the bias and the residual is 10−15.
  • The deafness lemma: a network equivariant under any element with a minus sign on κ₁ has zero go and nogo overlap on the decision readout. Verified as an exact zero at the tied DPA checkpoint.
  • The symmetry ledger, and the memory-stage ordering of the free seeds against their final outcome, are new measurements.
  • The λ = 14 tied-versus-untied experiment is now labeled as what it is, a control for the tie with a deliberate downward bias in the initialization; its depths are not evidence for the mechanism.
  • σ(n₁) after DPA is 3.58, not 3.68; the after-DPA schematic no longer names an element it cannot distinguish.
